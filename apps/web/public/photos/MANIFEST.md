  to avoid visual duplication when used together.

  composition/mood only; no visible logos or identifying signage that would misrepresent
  it as a specific US project.

  compositionally to read as US civil infrastructure; no country-identifying signage
  prominent in frame.
- No file required cropping/editing; all downloaded pre-cropped via Unsplash/Pexels CDN
  resize params (`w=`/`q=`) only — no source pixels altered beyond that.

### Second batch (18 photos, added 2026-09-04)

- **Race condition during sourcing:** this batch was independently researched twice in
  parallel by two background agent runs. Both wrote files to the same directory; whichever
  run wrote last won on disk, and at one point a stale completion notification caused a
  manifest edit to be applied that no longer matched the files actually on disk. Every row
  below was re-verified against the file that is **actually present in `public/photos/`
  right now** (dimensions/size confirmed via `sips`/`file`, content confirmed by opening
  each image) — not copied from any agent's self-report.
- **Rejected during final QA** (readable brand/equipment logos, or wrong subject, found by
  opening each file and looking, not by trusting a text description): a Hamburg
  "crane lifting a bridge girder" series (Wolfgang Weiser) recurred across every crop with
  a large readable **"LEONHARD WEISS"** banner and, in one frame, an **"EVERGREEN"** shipping
  container — rejected outright, not used, despite an earlier draft of this manifest
  incorrectly describing the logo as "small incidental text." A "surveyor with total
  station" candidate had a prominent **"John Deere"** logo on the bulldozer behind him —
  rejected. A "welder" candidate and an "excavator" candidate carried readable **brand
  names on the equipment itself** (a bright yellow **"VOLVO"** excavator, in particular, was
  the second run's actual pick, not merely a rejected also-ran as an earlier note claimed)
  — both rejected. A "hardhat detail" candidate turned out to be a hand holding a helmet
  with a visible **"UTILITY"** apparel-brand patch in frame — rejected. All five subjects
  were re-sourced with logo-free replacements (see below).
- `bridge-girder.jpg` — no true free-licensed "crawler crane mid-lift of a girder" frame
  without a readable logo was found after extensive search. Used instead: tower cranes over
  massive concrete bridge piers under construction at dusk, Kaliningrad, Russia — strong
  composition, correct subject family (bridge substructure + cranes), but not a literal
  girder lift. One crane in the far background carries a small readable "ZOOMLION"
  manufacturer nameplate on its jib — judged acceptable as incidental equipment-maker
  branding (same class as visible rental-fleet stickers), not a construction-firm logo
  misrepresenting the project.
- `surveyor.jpg` — replaced with a backlit silhouette shot (total station on tripod, hazy
  sunrise, rural fieldwork) specifically because the silhouette makes any brand markings
  unreadable. No location stated on the photo page.
- `welding.jpg` — restored to a plain sparks/dark-workshop shot with no equipment branding
  visible; no location stated.
- `earthworks.jpg` — restored to a wide daylight excavator shot (Nong Khai, Thailand) with
  no readable manufacturer branding, in place of the Volvo-branded frame.
- `hardhat-detail.jpg` — replaced with a clean studio product-style shot of a yellow hard
  hat on a dark surface: no hands, no face, no logo.
- `paving.jpg` — restored to a genuine asphalt-paving-machine-and-crew shot (Mascot,
  Australia) in place of a chip-seal/oil-truck photo that didn't match the "paving crew"
  brief as well.
- `tunnel-works.jpg` and `surveyor.jpg` have no location stated on their photo pages; no
  US-identifying or foreign-language signage is visible in frame.
- `concrete-pour.jpg` is by the same photographer as the existing `trades.jpg` (Ümit
  Tektaş), shot in Türkiye; no country-identifying signage in frame, generic heavy-civil
  composition.
- `safety-briefing.jpg` shows two engineers reviewing work at a precast/factory setting
  rather than a small-group toolbox talk on an active jobsite — the closest license-clear
  match after an extensive search that rejected several crowd photo-ops, large readable
  company logos, and non-US signage.
- `facade-install.jpg` was shot in Munich, Germany. Two earlier candidates (a Pexels
  Hamburg shot, a Lisbon Unsplash shot) had readable brand text/logos and were rejected;
  this shows curtain-wall panels mid-install with a lift, protective wrap, no branding.
- `mechanical-room.jpg` and `industrial-shell.jpg` have no stated location; verified as

- `finished-building.jpg` shows a small readable building-identity wordmark/shield on its
  own facade (the building's own signage, not a construction-firm logo) — judged
